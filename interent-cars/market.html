01 — The Market

Why traditional car buying is broken.

Three out of four car buyers start their journey online — then end up spending hours in a showroom, overpaying for financing, and leaving uncertain whether they got a fair deal.

The Intent Gap

Most buyers research online — then pay offline penalties.

Buyers invest hours comparing specs, reading reviews, and checking prices online — then surrender their leverage the moment they walk onto a lot. That transition is where billions in value leave the buyer's pocket and enter the dealership's.

$2,000+

Average amount overpaid by buyers on financing alone

Independent research consistently shows that when a car buyer accepts the dealer's in-house financing rather than independently arranged credit, they pay an average markup of $1,500–$3,000 over the life of the loan — entirely preventable with a transparent, pre-approved alternative.

The last industry still run on pressure sales
The Reality Today

Opaque. Coercive. Built to extract value from buyers.

Sticker prices bear little relation to transaction prices. Invoice pricing, holdback, dealer cash, and regional adjustments make the "real" price unknowable without insider knowledge — and that information asymmetry is the entire profit model.

Financing, add-ons, and warranty products generate more gross profit than the car itself in many transactions. Buyers who would never accept a mystery bill at a restaurant do so every day at a car dealership — because the system is engineered to make comparison difficult.

  • A.

    Opaque pricing and hidden markups

    Market-adjusted ADMs, freight charges, and documentation fees routinely add thousands above the advertised price.

  • B.

    Captive financing with inflated rates

    Dealer-arranged financing often carries a rate markup of 1–3% above the lending institution's approval — invisible to the buyer.

  • C.

    Inventory that doesn't match demand

    Physical lots stock what the dealer wants to move, not what buyers want — leading to pressure tactics and add-on packages.

  • D.

    No post-purchase transparency

    Once the deal is signed, most buyers have no way to assess whether they received fair market value — until it is too late.

A transaction this large deserves the same transparency you expect from every other market online.
